# Pack templates

Every artifact is a **DRAFT (designed, not run)** until the person says it ran. Never invent a number, a person, a wage, a customer or a result. Unknown stays unknown. Use the person's own words and the lab's wording where the lab supplies it, and name the lab file each part came from.

Evidence states (from the lab's workforce skill): VERIFIED, SUPPORTED, PROVISIONAL, INFERRED, UNKNOWN, CONTRADICTED. Never promote PROVISIONAL, INFERRED or UNKNOWN to verified.

## 4. Human-resources pack (design; protect employee data; HR decisions need a person's authorization)
Roles and who holds them; onboarding path (week 1, month 1, month 3); support plan and who the person can go to; review cadence (a conversation about goals, not a score); people KPIs with guardrails (from the lab's people-centered sections); how accommodations are asked for and recorded; how concerns are raised. Cite sections 27, 28, 29, 44 and 45 of `black-swan-labs-research-group.md` (staffing model, people operating model, relief system, people KPIs, quality guardrails) and quote its banner.

## 5. Grant-readiness checklist (a governance and evidence checklist, not a grant-writing shortcut)
1. Funder and program named; current rules retrieved from the funder (never from memory); deadline.
2. Eligibility gates (applicant, employer, trainees, match): each Yes / No / Unknown with its source. A failed mandatory gate stops the work.
3. Employer need evidence (documented, signed where required).
4. Training map (module 1) and assessment plan.
5. Outcome map: starting status, target, measure, source, date, evidence state. No fabricated wages, placements or retention.
6. Budget and match, as the funder defines them.
7. Rubric self-score using `workforce-grant-rubric-v1.yaml`, scored separately from eligibility.
8. Adversarial pass: attack each claim as the reviewer would.
9. Decision state: NOT_READY (more evidence needed) is not FAIL; BLOCKED means a mandatory condition fails; GRANT_READY only when mandatory gates and evidence all pass.
Rule (lab): a funded activity counts as training only when a worker acquires and demonstrates a competency. Strategy or automation work without worker capability development must not be relabeled as training.
Never say a grant will be won, and never say an application is ready when the evidence says it is not. A professional (grant writer, lawyer, accountant) reviews anything submitted.

## 6. Pack README (the person's cover page)
Their five answers in their words; what was fetched (file, status banner, date, hash from MANIFEST.json); what was drafted; what is unknown; what needs a person's decision; the next smallest step.

## 7. Growth measurement for people with disabilities or special needs  (NEW MATERIAL ADDED BY THIS SKILL, NOT FROM THE LAB)
The lab has no page on this (searched 2026-10-25). Treat this section as a design to review with disability-employment professionals (job coaches, vocational rehabilitation counselors, the worker and their chosen supporters) before use. Not legal, medical or clinical advice.

**Principles**
- Person first and strengths first. The worker takes part in setting their own goals; nothing is decided about them without them.
- Growth is measured against the person's own starting point, never against other workers. Never rank people.
- Collect the least data that does the job. Do not record diagnoses or health details in the pack. Get consent for any record, photo or video, and say who can see it.
- Accommodations and supports are normal working conditions, not a performance deficit. "More support needed today" is information, not failure; the plan allows plateaus and setbacks with a stated cause.
- Disability, privacy and employment rules (for example the ADA in the US, state and funder rules) apply: a qualified person checks the pack.

**What to measure (pick what Q4 chose; each gets a metric card with a guardrail)**
| Measure | How | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Skills demonstrated | A task broken into steps; a checklist of steps done correctly, observed on several days; passing rule written first | Do not shrink the task to make the number look better |
| Independence over time | Level of support per task (for example: full help, some prompts, reminder only, independent), recorded the same way each time | Support is faded only when the worker agrees and succeeds; never cut for cost |
| Personal goals | Goals the worker sets; rate each as much less / less / expected / more / much more than expected (an established method called goal attainment scaling; confirm the method with a practitioner) | The worker owns the goal and may change it |
| Attendance and retention | Days worked versus planned; months employed | Read with the worker's circumstances, not as a verdict |
| Hours and wages | Planned versus actual hours; wage rate and date; advancement | Never a target set without a baseline |
| Quality and safety | Same checks as any worker doing the task | Safety is never traded for speed |
| Employer results | Output, errors, time to complete, as the employer already measures them | Do not let the employer metric override the worker's own goals |

**Outputs for a funder**: a baseline for each worker (start date, starting support level, goals), a cadence (for example monthly review), and an outcomes table that reports each worker against their own baseline, with consent noted and personal details removed. Mark every number as measured or planned.

## 8. Schedule to measures (NEW; added after the first test, when the person said learners learn small things daily from a plan schedule and get OKRs and KPIs from it)
One table turns a plan schedule into learning, a daily check, a weekly KPI and a quarterly OKR key result:

| Schedule block | Competency learned (module) | Daily check (one observable thing) | Weekly KPI the learner can see | Quarterly OKR key result |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: one block, one competency. The daily check is something a person did or did not do that day, recorded the same way each time. The weekly KPI is the count or rate of daily checks passed, shown in plain words (and pictures if needed). The key result is an outcome, not a task. Every KPI has a baseline ("measure first") and a paired guardrail. Each learner sees their own numbers against their own baseline and goals, never against another learner. Fill rows only from the person's real schedule; unknown stays UNKNOWN.
